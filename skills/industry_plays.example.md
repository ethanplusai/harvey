# Industry plays (example)

Copy this file to `skills/industry_plays.md` and rewrite it for your firm, or
let `harvey train <your-site>` draft one from your website. Harvey uses it
when `product.offering` is `services`.

Each `## Industry` section is one industry. For a prospect, Harvey uses the
first section whose `match:` words (or title) appear in the company's
industry, or failing that in its description or the tools on its site.
`roles:` are job titles whose openings suggest a company there may need you;
profiling looks for them on the company's job board. Everything else in a
section goes to the writer and reply handler as written.

These examples are starting points, not facts about your firm. Replace the
offers with what you actually do, and the proof with what you can actually
show. Delete the sections you don't need. Anything you leave here, the writer
may repeat.

The first four are written for a software, AI and product development firm.
The last two show the same file working for a business consulting firm and an
accounting firm.

## Insurance
match: insurance, insurer, carrier, mga, managing general agent, insurance broker, insurtech, underwriting
roles: claims processor, claims adjuster, claims examiner, underwriting assistant, policy administrator

**What we offer them:**
- Claims intake and triage automation: first notice of loss from email, forms and PDFs into the claims system, routed by type and severity
- Document extraction for claims and submissions (loss runs, ACORD forms, invoices) with a person reviewing the exceptions
- AI agents that draft adjuster notes and customer updates for a person to approve
- Integration with the core system they already run (Guidewire, Duck Creek, Applied Epic or a legacy one) rather than replacing it
- Broker and policyholder portals

**Problems this industry often has (not proof about any one company):**
- Claims arrive by email and PDF and are rekeyed by hand
- Many claims or underwriting roles open at once: manual volume growing faster than hiring
- A core system that is hard to change, so every new channel becomes another manual step

**Proof:**
- (Your insurance case studies, as you can state them publicly. If none, delete this line and Harvey claims none.)

**Don't say:**
- Anything about their loss ratios, claims backlog or fraud rates: you can't see them
- That automation will cut staff; buyers and their regulators are sensitive to it
- That an AI system makes claim decisions on its own

## Accounting firms
match: accounting firm, cpa, cpa firm, chartered accountants, bookkeeping, tax firm, audit firm
roles: staff accountant, senior accountant, bookkeeper, tax associate, accounts payable specialist

**What we offer them:**
- Middleware that connects the tools they already use (QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite, Sage, practice management, payroll, tax software) so client data moves without rekeying
- Document capture and reconciliation: bank statements, receipts and invoices into the ledger, with exceptions for a person
- A client portal for requests, uploads and status, instead of email threads
- Automations sized for tax season, so the busy months don't need temporary staff

**Problems this industry often has (not proof about any one company):**
- The same client data typed into three or four systems
- Chasing clients for documents by email every season
- Hiring for staff accountant roles that stay open for months

**Proof:**
- (Your accounting-firm work, as you can state it publicly.)

**Don't say:**
- Anything that sounds like accounting or tax advice
- Anything about their clients, whose data they must keep confidential

## Fintech
match: fintech, payments, lending, neobank, banking as a service, wealthtech, regtech, credit union
roles: compliance analyst, kyc analyst, aml analyst, fraud analyst, risk analyst, backend engineer

**What we offer them:**
- KYC, KYB and AML workflow automation: case queues, document checks, evidence trails for review
- Integrations with banking and payment providers (for example Plaid, Stripe, card issuing and core banking APIs)
- Risk and decisioning services, with a human in the loop and an audit log
- Engineering for audit readiness: access controls, logging and change management that SOC 2 and PCI DSS assessors look for

**Problems this industry often has (not proof about any one company):**
- Compliance analysts working cases by hand as volume grows
- Every new partner bank or market needs another integration
- An audit or certification date coming up with gaps in the evidence

**Proof:**
- (Your fintech work, as you can state it publicly.)

**Don't say:**
- That you make them compliant, or that they are not compliant
- Anything about their fraud losses, regulators or enforcement

## SaaS
match: saas, software company, b2b software, software as a service, devtools
roles: product designer, ux designer, ux researcher, frontend engineer, full stack engineer, product manager, machine learning engineer

**What we offer them:**
- UI/UX research and product design: finding where users get stuck, then fixing those flows
- Design systems, so a growing product stays consistent across teams
- Product development: new features, modules or a new product with a dedicated team
- Staff augmentation: senior engineers or designers embedded in their team while they hire
- AI features and agents inside their product, and making their API usable by AI agents

**Problems this industry often has (not proof about any one company):**
- The roadmap is longer than the team
- Design, engineering or ML roles open for months
- A product that grew feature by feature and now feels inconsistent

**Proof:**
- (Your SaaS work, as you can state it publicly.)

**Don't say:**
- That their product is hard to use, unless they said so
- That you'll replace their team
