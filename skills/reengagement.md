# Re-engagement: "Not Interested", Gone Dark and Champion Departures

"Not interested" is usually a reflex, not a verdict. It's about half of all cold-outreach objections and most often means bad timing, risk aversion or a weak opener, not genuine disinterest. Many buyers say no several times before saying yes. The job is to diagnose *which* "no" this is, respond to that, and never burn the relationship.

**Opt-outs always win.** Anyone who asks to stop, unsubscribe or not be contacted is never re-engaged, whatever this skill says. Everything below applies only to people who haven't opted out.

## Diagnose Before Responding

- **Reflex "no"** (instant, to a first touch): they rejected the approach, not the offer. The fix is a sharper, more specific hook, never repeating the same message.
- **Stated reason** (happy with current provider / no budget / bad timing): each has its own play (table below).
- **Gone dark** (engaged, then silence): priorities shifted. Silence is rarely a hard no. Come back only with something *new*.

## Live "Not Interested": Listen → Ask → Solve → Confirm

Validate without arguing, ask **one** open question to surface the real reason ("Is it the timing, or just not relevant right now?"), then answer that reason. If it's a genuine no, thank them and exit cleanly. A professional exit is an asset: there may be a better moment in six months.

## Objection-Specific Plays

| They say | It usually means | Play |
|---|---|---|
| "Happy with our current provider" | Switching feels risky, not that they're delighted | Don't attack the incumbent. Ask what would have to break for them to look around; plant one specific differentiator |
| "No budget" | Not a priority *yet*, or the wrong quarter | Ask when budgets are set; mention a smaller entry point if the product has one |
| "Bad timing / later" | Genuine, or a soft brush-off | Pin it down: "Would [specific month] be better to reconnect?" |
| "Send me some info" | Often a polite exit | Offer one *specific* thing tied to their situation, with one question that invites a reply |
| "Not the right person" | A navigation gift | Ask who is. A referral from a colleague is the warmest intro there is |

## Gone-Dark Re-engagement

- Every touch needs a **new angle**: a fresh buying signal, a relevant result, a trigger event or a direct question. "Just checking in" and "bumping this up" add nothing.
- **Trigger events are the best re-entry**: a raise, a leadership change, a hiring push, a product launch. They make the message about *them*.
- If the conversation died over a specific issue (price, a missing feature, timing), reach back only when something has actually changed.
- Space touches 4-7 days apart at minimum. Several emails in a few days reads as desperation.

## The Breakup Message

The breakup is the last touch, sent **once per deal, ever**. It works because it's honest: you really are closing the file. Acknowledge the earlier messages, say without guilt that you haven't heard back, offer two clean options (a next step, or "I'll close this out"), one question. No guilt trips, no passive aggression. If there's no reply, stop.

## Champion Departure

When the contact has left the company, that's two openings, not zero:
1. **The account**: their successor inherits the problem, and new leaders re-evaluate vendors in their first months. Ask, politely, who now owns it.
2. **The person**: their new company may be a warm prospect, since they already know the offer. Note it for the owner; don't follow them uninvited.

## Patterns Are Upstream Problems

If the same objection keeps coming from the same kind of business, it's a message-market-fit signal, not an objection to keep handling. Flag it for the owner so it can be saved as a learning (`harvey learnings --add`).

## Honesty Rules

- Never argue with an objection, invent urgency, or claim scarcity that doesn't exist.
- If the honest read is "this lead is done", say so. Closing a conversation with a clear reason keeps the pipeline honest.
