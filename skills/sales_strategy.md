# Sales Strategy: Matching the Approach to the Buyer

Every reply and every follow-up should trace back to something real: a fact from the prospect's own site, a buying signal, something they said, or the product's actual value. Generic sales advice is noise. This skill is about choosing the right move for where *this* buyer is right now.

## Pick the Approach by Buyer State

| Buyer state | Approach | Core move |
|---|---|---|
| Doesn't feel the problem yet | **SPIN discovery** | Ask questions that surface the problem and make its cost concrete |
| Knows the problem, comfortable with the status quo | **Challenger reframe** | Share one specific insight that changes how they see it |
| Actively evaluating options | **Differentiate + qualify** | Win on *their* stated criteria; qualify before investing more |

Mix them: SPIN for discovery, a light MEDDIC check for qualification, Challenger for differentiation. Consistency matters more than which framework.

## SPIN in a Reply

1. **Situation**: keep these minimal. Harvey has already read their site, so don't ask what you can look up.
2. **Problem**: "How are you handling X today? Where does that break down?"
3. **Implication**: make the cost concrete. "What does that delay do to Y?" Urgency is *built* by the buyer's own answer, never asserted by Harvey.
4. **Need-payoff**: let them say the value. "If that ran itself, what would it free your team to do?" Buyers trust their own conclusions more than a seller's claims.

In email, ask **one** of these per message, never a questionnaire.

## Challenger, Honestly

- The real competitor in most deals is **no decision**: the status quo wins more often than any vendor.
- The move: lead with one *specific, evidence-backed* observation about their business they may not have considered, which leads naturally to the product's differentiator.
- Build the insight only from facts Harvey actually has (their site, signals, their own words). If the evidence can't support a confident insight, fall back to a SPIN question instead of bluffing.

## Light MEDDIC Check (deals past "engaged")

Four questions worth answering before pushing for a close:
- **Metrics**: what measurable outcome do they expect?
- **Economic buyer**: who actually signs? Is it the person replying?
- **Decision process**: what steps or approvals stand between "interested" and "yes"?
- **Champion**: who inside wants this to happen, and does Harvey make them look good?

An unanswered check is a question for the next message or the call, never a guess.

## Buyer Psychology (what actually moves B2B decisions)

- **Loss aversion**: people act 2-3x more readily to avoid a loss than to capture a gain. Frame around the cost of staying as-is, but only with facts you have. Never invent numbers.
- **Status quo bias**: "staying as-is is a choice that costs you X" beats "we're better than Y".
- **Career risk**: the person replying is risking their own credibility. Lower *their* risk: proof from similar businesses, a small first step (audit, pilot, short call), material they can forward internally.
- **Anchoring**: the first frame or number in a conversation pulls everything after it. Set frames deliberately.

## Influence Principles: Honest Use Only

| Principle | Legitimate use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give real value first: a specific observation, a useful benchmark | Strings-attached "gifts" |
| Commitment | Small first steps that naturally precede bigger ones | Trapping someone with their own words |
| Social proof | Results from *similar* businesses that are in the product knowledge | Invented or exaggerated case studies |
| Authority | Real credentials and third-party facts, in a neutral tone | Borrowed or fake authority |
| Scarcity | Real constraints only, stated as planning | Fake deadlines or invented limits |

## Pipeline Patience

At any moment only a small slice of a market is actively buying; a much larger group isn't thinking about the problem yet but could be. "Not now" is a scheduling problem, not a rejection. Treat it that way (see the re-engagement skill).
